### Picture a Scientist

The documentary Picture a Scientist highlights the inequalities and challenges that women in science still face today. Through the stories of biologist Nancy Hopkins, chemist Raychelle Burks, and geologist Jane Willenbring, we discover powerful testimonies about the different forms of harassment they endured — moral, sexual, and even physical. These accounts show how difficult it can be for a woman to gain recognition and respect in a field still marked by inequality.

Before the screening, the presenter asked us why we had come. For me, it was out of curiosity and a desire to better understand the place of women in the scientific world — a topic that is rarely discussed but deserves our full attention.

This documentary deeply moved me because of the strength of the testimonies and the determination to change mindsets. Beyond raising awareness, it also offers thoughtful perspectives on how to make research more fair and inclusive. It reminds us that science can only truly progress when it embraces diversity and respect.

### L’Hiver Sera Chaud

The play L’Hiver Sera Chaud highlights the contradictions and challenges our society faces today: the ecological crisis, social inequalities, and civic engagement. Through a performance that is both humorous and powerful, the actors shared testimonies about their doubts, frustrations, and desire to act collectively for a fairer future.

I found this play particularly moving for its ability to combine humor and reflection. It encourages us to question our habits, our relationship with the environment, and solidarity — all while maintaining a hopeful tone. The interactive format, which involved the audience directly in several scenes, was also very relevant and engaging.
